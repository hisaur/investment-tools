# Overlooked Pockets — Where To Actually Fish

These are the corners of public equity markets where retail and small funds can still find an edge because index funds, sell-side analysts, and mega-funds structurally can't or won't look there. Each pocket has an academic paper or a legendary practitioner's track record supporting it.

## 1. Spin-offs (Greenblatt's main hunting ground)

**The edge**: When a conglomerate spins off a subsidiary, shares of the spinco get dumped indiscriminately by institutions who can't hold small-caps, don't know the business, or don't want orphan positions. Prices decouple from fundamentals for 6-12 months.

**Evidence**:
- Greenblatt (1997) *You Can Be a Stock Market Genius* — documents ~10-15% annualized excess return
- Cusatis, Miles, Woolridge (1993) JFE — 25% two-year outperformance vs. matched controls
- McConnell & Ovtchinnikov (2004) JFE — persistence through 2002

**How to find them**:
- SEC EDGAR form search for `10-12B` (spin-off registration statement)
- `https://efts.sec.gov/LATEST/search-index?q=&forms=10-12B`
- Stock Spinoffs Newsletter, Spinoff Research

**Filters to apply**:
- Market cap of spinco < 25% of parent pre-spin
- Insider ownership of spinco > 5%
- Management incentivized with spinco equity
- ≥ 3 months since spin completion (let forced selling finish)
- Price at least 15% below 30-day VWAP post-spin

## 2. Post-bankruptcy emergers

**The edge**: Fresh equity holders of a reorganized company are often distressed debt funds who sell immediately. The equity trades with zero analyst coverage for 12-24 months while the business quietly fixes itself.

**Evidence**:
- Eberhart, Altman, Aggarwal (1999) J. Finance — 24.6% excess return in year 1 post-emergence
- Ycharts and Altman NYU studies — consistent outperformance ~2000-2020

**How to find them**:
- PACER / bankruptcy court dockets (Chapter 11 plan confirmations)
- SEC Form 8-K "Item 1.03 Bankruptcy or Receivership"
- 10-K fresh-start accounting disclosures (pattern: "as of [effective date], we applied fresh-start reporting")
- Key list maintained by Reorg Research, Debtwire (paywalled but mentioned in news)

**Filters**:
- Emerged in last 36 months
- Net debt reduced ≥ 50% post-emergence
- New management installed
- Any Form 4 insider buying post-emergence

## 3. Mega-cap & Large-cap temporary dislocations ("Fallen Angels")

**The edge**: Mega-cap and large-cap stocks ($10B–$200B+) are widely covered, but market overreactions to temporary headwinds (legal overhangs, cyclical downturns, temporary earnings misses, management transitions) can create high-margin-of-safety entry points in world-class compounders.

**Evidence**:
- Buffett's investments in American Express (Salad Oil scandal), Wells Fargo, Apple, and Bank of America during distress.
- Damodaran valuation research — market over-discounting temporary cyclical dips in high-ROIC mega-caps.

**How to find them**:
- Screen for mega-caps / large-caps trading near 52-week lows or >30% off high-water mark with intact FCF.
- High ROIC (≥ 15%) coupled with temporary depressed EV/EBIT or elevated FCF yield (≥ 8%).

**Filters**:
- Market cap ≥ $300M (microcaps <$300M excluded per strategy rules).
- Core moat / competitive advantage remains intact despite short-term headwinds.
- Balance sheet distress zone avoided (Altman Z > 1.8).
- Positive FCF generation throughout the downturn.

## 4. Insider buying clusters (Form 4 signal)

**The edge**: Insiders (CEO, CFO, board) have information advantages. Purchases (not sales) cluster when management sees near-term upside the market hasn't priced. Multiple insiders buying at once is materially more informative than one.

**Evidence**:
- Jeng, Metrick, Zeckhauser (2003) — insider buys beat market by ~6% annualized
- Cohen, Malloy, Pomorski (2012) JF — "opportunistic" insider trades (not routine grants) add 8%+ alpha
- Lakonishok & Lee (2001) — cluster effects (2+ insiders) strongest signal

**How to find them**:
- SEC EDGAR Form 4 search: `https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&type=4&dateb=&owner=include&count=40&action=getcompany`
- OpenInsider.com — aggregated Form 4 data, free
- Fintel.io — aggregated Form 4 + 13D/13G

**Filters**:
- ≥ 2 insiders buying in same 30-day window
- Each purchase ≥ $50k (exclude token purchases)
- NOT Rule 10b5-1 pre-scheduled (look for "P" code, not "G" or option exercises)
- Buyer ranks CEO / CFO / Chairman / Board (exclude junior VPs)

## 5. Activist 13D / 13G filings

**The edge**: When a respected activist takes a >5% position and files a 13D, they're publicly committing to push for change. Their presence alone often drives re-rating.

**Names to track (as of 2026)**:
- Carl Icahn (Icahn Enterprises)
- David Einhorn (Greenlight Capital)
- Bill Ackman (Pershing Square)
- Mohnish Pabrai (Pabrai Funds)
- Michael Burry (Scion Capital)
- Seth Klarman (Baupost)
- Dan Loeb (Third Point)
- Nelson Peltz (Trian)
- Jeffrey Ubben (ValueAct / Inclusive Capital)
- Elliott Management

**How to find them**:
- SEC EDGAR Schedule 13D search by filer CIK
- `https://efts.sec.gov/LATEST/search-index?q=&forms=SC+13D`
- Dataroma.com — aggregates 13F + 13D filings for famous value investors

**Caveat**: Don't blindly copy. Einhorn was wrong on Allied Capital. Ackman lost on Valeant. Use the filing as a starting point to do your own work.

## 6. Forced selling events

**The edge**: When a stock is kicked out of an index (Russell, S&P), passive funds must sell regardless of fundamentals. This creates mispricings for 2-8 weeks.

**Evidence**:
- Chen, Noronha, Singal (2004) J. Finance — index addition/deletion price effects
- Harris & Gurel (1986) — original documentation

**How to find them**:
- Russell annual reconstitution announcements (June each year)
- S&P Dow Jones Indices press releases
- Follow deletions more than additions (mispricing is bigger on the sell side)

## 7. Sum-of-parts (SOTP) discounts

**The edge**: Conglomerates with multiple distinct businesses often trade at a discount to the sum of those businesses' standalone values. A break-up, spin-off, or SOTP re-rating closes the gap.

**Examples (historical)**:
- John Malone's Liberty entities
- Joe Lewis' Tavistock
- Rupert Murdoch's News Corp pre-split

**How to find them**:
- 10-K segment reporting (Notes to Financial Statements)
- Screen for companies with ≥ 3 reportable segments and negative segment correlation

## 8. Post-dividend-cut overreaction

**The edge**: When a company cuts or suspends its dividend, income-focused funds are forced sellers regardless of whether the business is fundamentally impaired. Short-term price drop often exceeds the change in intrinsic value.

**Evidence**:
- Michaely, Thaler, Womack (1995) J. Finance — dividend initiation/omission announcement effects
- Retail observations: ex-dividend-cut portfolios with intact FCF have beaten the market historically

**How to find them**:
- 8-K filings with "dividend" keyword
- Bloomberg dividend calendar (paywalled but news reports pick up cuts)
- Any stock down >15% on a dividend-cut headline where Piotroski stayed ≥ 7

## 9. Non-covered foreign listings

**The edge**: Stocks primarily listed on non-US exchanges (Hong Kong, Tokyo, Toronto, London) with secondary ADRs are often neglected by US analysts even though the primary listing is liquid.

**Names to look for**:
- Berkshire-style compounders in Japan (Fanuc, Shin-Etsu, Keyence) during drawdowns
- Canadian resource juniors post-commodity crashes
- Hong Kong real estate names trading below NAV

**Caveats**:
- Currency risk — hedge or size smaller
- Governance risk (especially China/HK) — requires extra skepticism on accounting
- Liquidity — avoid if ADR average volume < $500k/day

## 10. Rights offerings / warrants creating dislocations

**The edge**: Rights offerings cause short-term selling as holders choose not to subscribe. Warrants expire out-of-the-money and get delisted — the underlying equity sometimes has a separate mispricing.

**How to find them**:
- 8-K "Unregistered Sales of Equity" filings
- S-1/A amendments for rights offerings
- OTC warrant expirations

## 11. Cash-box and NCAV (Graham net-nets)

**The edge**: Companies trading below net current asset value (NCAV = Current Assets − Total Liabilities). Pure balance-sheet arbitrage, working capital > market cap. Basically extinct in the US mid/large-cap space post-1990 but occasionally still appears in microcaps and post-crash periods.

**Evidence**:
- Oppenheimer (1986) Financial Analysts Journal — 29.4% annualized return on NCAV basket 1970-1983
- Montier (2008) GMO white paper — replicated globally, still worked
- Carlisle, Montier books — mechanical NCAV strategy continues to beat market in out-of-sample tests

**Filters**:
- Current assets > total liabilities × 1.33 (Graham's 2/3 rule)
- Market cap < NCAV × 2/3
- Positive operating cash flow in 2 of last 3 years (not a cash-burning husk)
- No going-concern language in auditor report

## 12. Orphaned tracking stocks / holdcos

Rare but extremely mispriced when they appear. Liberty Broadband vs. Charter, Altaba vs. Alibaba, etc.

## Meta-note

**None of these pockets print money anymore the way they did in 1990.** Academic research (Hou-Xue-Zhang 2020, McLean-Pontiff 2016) shows that anomaly returns decay ~58% post-publication as capital floods in. The surviving edge comes from:
1. Combining pockets (e.g., spin-off + insider cluster + microcap)
2. Patience (holding through 2-3 year realization periods)
3. Rejecting value traps ruthlessly (see red flags reference)

Expect a realistic post-cost Sharpe of 0.5-0.7 from a diversified deep-value portfolio, not the 1.5+ in the back-tested literature.
