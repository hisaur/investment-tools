# Buffett / Munger Qualitative Gates

Quantitative filters (Piotroski, Altman, Magic Formula) tell you the business looks healthy. Qualitative gates tell you whether a healthy business is *worth owning*. Use these as pass/fail decisions in Stage 2, not as essay prompts.

## Gate 1: Is the business within your circle of competence?

**Test**: Can you explain in 2 sentences what the company does, how it makes money, and what could go wrong, to an intelligent 12-year-old?

**Pass**: Yes. You understand the revenue model, customer dynamics, and primary risks.
**Fail**: No. Technology you can't assess (deep pharma, leading-edge semis, complex financial engineering), or a business in an industry you've never worked in or studied.

**Buffett quote**: "Risk comes from not knowing what you're doing."

If you fail this gate, it goes to the Too Hard pile immediately. Do not proceed to valuation. The most expensive mistake is buying something you don't understand just because the numbers look good.

## Gate 2: Is there a durable competitive advantage (a moat)?

**Test**: Identify the SPECIFIC source of the moat. One of:
- **Brand** (consumers pay premium, switching costs are emotional)
- **Network effects** (each user makes product more valuable — Visa, Facebook)
- **Switching costs** (technical lock-in, regulatory lock-in, learned behavior — Microsoft Office, ERP systems)
- **Cost advantages** (scale, location, process, resource access — Costco, Walmart, BHP)
- **Regulatory / patent** (legal exclusivity, licenses — utilities, pipelines, some pharma)
- **Efficient scale** (market only supports a few players — pipelines, railroads, stock exchanges)

**Pass**: You can name one specifically, and provide evidence it's still widening (market share gains, pricing power ≥ inflation, low customer churn).
**Fail**: No identifiable moat, OR the moat exists but is narrowing (lost market share, deflationary pricing, accelerating churn).

**Not a moat**:
- "Hard work" (every company works hard)
- "Culture" (real but not measurable from outside)
- "Technology" (unless patent-protected)
- "First-mover advantage" (often evaporates)
- "Customer service" (cited by every failed company's CEO)

## Gate 3: Is management honest and competent?

**Three subtests**:

### 3a. Capital allocation
Read the last 5 years of capital allocation decisions (from 10-K cash flow statements):
- FCF → dividends + buybacks + acquisitions + reinvestment + debt paydown
- Did each bucket create or destroy shareholder value?
- Were buybacks done below intrinsic value, or above?
- Were acquisitions integrated successfully or taken as goodwill impairments later?

**Pass**: Consistent allocation to high-return use of cash. Buybacks at reasonable prices. Acquisitions that earned their cost of capital.
**Fail**: Chronic empire-building, buybacks at all-time highs, repeated goodwill impairments, dividend increases while taking on debt.

### 3b. Candor in shareholder letters
Read the last 3 CEO letters (in 10-K or standalone). Score 1-10:
- Admits mistakes?
- Discusses failed initiatives in concrete terms?
- Gives specific forward guidance?
- Uses plain English, not jargon?
- Talks about the downside, not just the upside?

**Pass**: ≥ 6/10. Real candor, real humility.
**Fail**: ≤ 4/10. Generic corporate speak, only good news, no mention of mistakes.

### 3c. Insider ownership and behavior
- **Ownership**: Insider holdings > 5% of outstanding = skin in the game
- **Recent activity**: Net buying or net selling in last 12 months (from Form 4)
- **Compensation structure**: From DEF 14A — cash-heavy (bad) or stock-heavy with long vesting (good)?

**Pass**: Insiders own ≥ 5%, net buyers in last year, compensation tied to long-term stock.
**Fail**: Insiders own < 1%, chronic selling, compensation dominated by cash bonuses with short-term targets.

## Gate 4: Does the business have favorable economics?

**Test**: Is this a good business at a fair price, or a fair business at a good price? (Buffett: "Much better to buy a wonderful company at a fair price than a fair company at a wonderful price.")

**Good business markers**:
- ROIC > 15% over full cycle
- Gross margin ≥ 35% and stable
- Net margin ≥ 10%
- Pricing power: can raise prices ≥ inflation without losing volume
- Low capital intensity: reinvestment < 30% of operating cash flow
- Favorable secular trends: TAM growing ≥ GDP

**Pass**: ≥ 4 of the 6 markers.
**Fail**: ≤ 2 of the 6 markers.

Asset plays can fail this gate and still be buyable — they're not being valued on their economics, they're being valued on their balance sheet. But be explicit about which case you're making.

## Gate 5: Is the price right?

**Test**: Computed from `valuation-methods.md`. Margin of safety (probability-weighted fair value vs. current price):

- ≥ 50%: Pass, strong
- 30-50%: Pass
- 15-30%: Marginal pass, small position only
- 0-15%: Watchlist
- < 0%: Fail

**Pass/fail depends on your required margin of safety**, which depends on:
- Business quality (better businesses need lower MoS — Buffett bought KO at ~fair value)
- Predictability (cyclicals and commodities need 40%+, consumer staples can be 15%+)
- Balance sheet strength (leveraged companies need bigger cushion)

## Gate 6: Is it free from binary risks?

**Test**: Is there ANY single event that could permanently impair ≥ 50% of the equity value?

Examples:
- FDA decision (pharma)
- Court ruling (litigation overhang)
- Single customer contract renewal (concentration)
- Commodity price moves (resource companies)
- Regulatory change (utilities, banks, crypto)
- Refinancing (leveraged names)

**Pass**: No single event kills the thesis. Even a bad outcome would reduce value by < 30%.
**Fail**: Binary risk exists. This is not a value investment, it's an options bet.

## Gate 7: Is the time horizon realistic?

**Test**: How long until the thesis plays out?

- **< 6 months**: Probably not a value investment — trading, special situation, or event-driven
- **6-18 months**: Reasonable for special situations, spin-offs, post-earnings overreactions
- **18-36 months**: Core value territory — business cycle normalization, multiple re-rating
- **3-5 years**: Compounding story, earnings growth
- **> 5 years**: Requires very high conviction and explicit patience

**Pass**: You can articulate a plausible 18-36 month path to realization.
**Fail**: "Someday the market will recognize this" without a concrete catalyst.

## The scoring rule

Of the 7 gates, Buffett-style requires **Pass on all 7** for a major position. Greenblatt's Magic Formula is more mechanical and tolerates Fail on gates 1 and 3 (you hold a diversified basket). Pabrai requires Pass on all 7 for concentration.

Our recommended rule: **Pass on 5 of 7 for a starter position, 7 of 7 for a full-sized one.**

## Meta-rule

**Gate 7 must always pass** regardless of the others. A permanent capital impairment risk is a dealbreaker. The quote to keep on the wall: "Rule number one: don't lose money. Rule number two: don't forget rule number one." — Warren Buffett
